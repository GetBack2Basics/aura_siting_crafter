Geotechnical stability and risk management – Development on the TSF and the

considered. 
Utility infrastructure – Viability and sequencing of the provision of utilities, 
particularly to the West Lake sub-precinct, will need further investigation.

Water cycle management
Water cycle management is a key consideration 
for future development within the Precinct. Diega 
Creek and Cockle Creek are the two key waterways 
located within the Precinct. Existing stormwater 
management within the Precinct is through

disturbed land. Water management infrastructure

dams, spillways and drainage systems. Cockle

from the Precinct, receiving flows from upstream 
tributaries, natural flow paths and water directed

	•

	•

resilience and place outcomes.
	•
Maintain natural drainage function, groundwater 
processes and recharge where feasible.
	•

Road network, freight and access
Road network and access is influenced by 
the Precinct’s legacy as former coal mine 
operations. Historic mining operations have shaped 
both the physical terrain and created a framework 
that continues to inform movement and access in 
the post mining context. Former haul routes and 
access tracks, originally designed to support 
extraction, processing and transport activities,

mining land use movement network.

multi-layered, combining infrastructure planning 
and staging through demand management and 
integration with land use outcomes. To facilitate 
efficient movement within and around the Precinct, 
the road network of sub-arterial, collector and local 
roads is structured as a clear hierarchy, linking 
developable land to the broader regional network 
to minimise reliance on the external local network. 
Subject to further investigation, a potential new 
east–west crossing of Cockle Creek is identified as 
an opportunity to strengthen network resilience, 
unlock alternative freight access that bypasses 
constrained local roads (Racecourse Road, Railway 
Street and Rhondda Road) and improve 
connectivity to Cockle Creek Station.
Teralba is directly serviced by Teralba and 
Booragul Stations and forms a natural anchor for 
enhanced pedestrian and cycling connectivity to 
the wider Precinct via the Main Northern railway 
corridor and the future active transport network.
