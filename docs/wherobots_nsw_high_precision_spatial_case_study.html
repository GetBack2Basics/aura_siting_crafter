Executive Summary

Evaluating site suitability for critical national infrastructure—such as sovereign AI data centers, battery energy storage systems (BESS), and renewable energy hubs—traditionally requires weeks of manual, desktop-bound GIS geoprocessing. Desktop workflows frequently struggle with continental-scale datasets, split coordinate reference systems (CRS), and lack reproducible version control.

This case study demonstrates how AURA Siting Crafter implemented a cloud-native, distributed spatial lakehouse architecture using Apache Sedona and Wherobots Cloud across 15.94 million authoritative spatial geometries in all 8 Australian jurisdictions.

By enforcing a two-phase ingestion lifecycle, standardizing on EPSG:7844 (GDA2020), and decoupling heavy geometric overlays from lightweight client-side scoring, total batch compute spend was reduced to ~$1.03 AUD (~US$0.69) per full national batch run, with interactive scenario modeling offloaded 100% to DuckDB-WASM at $0.00 cloud compute cost.

1. The Challenge: State-Wide Scale & Spatial Fragmentation

Analyzing site suitability across New South Wales and nationwide involves intersecting massive, heterogeneous public datasets:

  • 15.42M National Cadastral Parcels (Geoscape & G-NAF): Property boundaries, legal lot/plans, and address points.
  • 368k ABS 2021 Meshblocks: Land use designations for residential and sensitive receptor setback enforcement.
  • NSW SEED Portal & Spatial Services Feeds: BioNet Biodiversity Values (BV) Map, Koala Habitat (KHIB), Strahler Stream Orders (1–7+), RFS Bush Fire Prone Land (BFPL), and Declared Tailings Storage Facilities (TSFs).
  • High-Voltage Transmission Networks: Transgrid ≥132kV lines, 330kV/500kV substations, and declared Renewable Energy Zones (REZs).

The Multi-Zone Projection Trap

Traditional Australian GIS pipelines frequently project data into regional UTM zones (e.g. MGA Zone 54 EPSG:7854, Zone 55 EPSG:7855, Zone 56 EPSG:7856). Across NSW and nationwide, this approach causes:

  1. Severe distortion and geometry shearing at UTM zone boundaries.
  2. Complicated multi-zone ETL pipelines requiring dataset splitting and stitching.
  3. Inability to perform seamless cross-state spatial joins.
The Solution
Standardizing all persistent tables and spatial joins on EPSG:7844 (GDA2020 Geographic 2D). When calculating precise metric buffers (such as statutory 30m riparian buffers or 100m bushfire APZs), geometries are dynamically reprojected to equal-area EPSG:3112 (GDA2020 Geoscience Australia Albers Equal Area), buffered in meters, and stored back in EPSG:7844.

2. The Sequential Geospatial Lifecycle

Following the Wherobots & Antigravity Engineering Playbook, our pipeline enforces a strict three-stage lifecycle:

1

Stage 1: Spatial Planning

EPSG:7844 baseline definition, statutory safety buffers (30m riparian, 20m pipelines, 100m APZ), fallback schema design.

2

Stage 2: Two-Phase ETL

Phase 1 (Download, Sanitize, Repair, Project First) followed by Phase 2 (Complex Buffers & Overlays).

3

Stage 3: Multi-Criteria Siting

Centroid proximity matrix, continuous decay curves, and decoupled client-side slider re-scoring.

Stage 1: Spatial Planning & Data Alignment

  • Defined unified coordinate reference systems (EPSG:7844 for storage, EPSG:3112 for metric calculations).
  • Established the Geoscape National Cadastre as the single source of truth nationwide, eliminating duplicate state DCDB parcel databases.
  • Defined statutory safety buffers: 30m riparian, 20m high-pressure pipeline easements, 100m bushfire APZ, 500m acoustic compliance setback.

Stage 2: Two-Phase Spatial ETL (Download & Setup First)

  • Phase 1 (Data Download, Setup & Projection Standardization First): Raw vector feeds from 18 state and national APIs are harvested, coordinate-sanitized (_clean_coordinates), repaired (ST_MakeValid), normalized to EPSG:7844, and materialized directly into partitioned Apache Iceberg (Havasu) tables (org_catalog.fgsdb.*).
  • Phase 2 (Complex Spatial Processing & Overlays): Topological clipping, equal-area metric buffering, ST_Difference developable overlays, and spatial joins execute against the pre-materialized base tables.

Stage 3: Multi-Criteria Siting & Querying

  • Centroid-Based Proximity: Distance measurements to power lines, rail, and water outfalls use parcel centroids (ST_Centroid(geom)), preventing 0.0m artifacts from boundary intersections.
  • Continuous Decay Curves: Replaced crude step-functions with smooth piecewise linear and sigmoidal decay functions (Spower, Ssensitive, Swater).

4. Cost Optimization & Compute Economics

During the national scale-up, total batch compute spend across dozens of headless pipeline runs on Wherobots Cloud was $24.13 USD.

Batch Run Cost Allocation

  • Regional NSW Ingestion & Repair (~14 runs): $9.65 USD
  • Spatial Joins & Net Developable Overlays (~11 runs): $7.59 USD
  • National Spatial SQL Benchmarks (~6 runs): $4.14 USD
  • Automated QA & Regression Passes (~4 runs): $2.75 USD
  • Last Full Pipeline Run: $0.69 USD across 15.91M national geometries.
  • Total Batch Execution Cost: $24.13 USD cumulative spend across ~35 automated batch runs (averaging $0.69 USD per full run).

Key Architectural Lessons from Wherobots Platform Analytics

  1. 80% Active / 20% Idle Compute: Platform utilization metrics showed that 20% of developmental spend arose from idle resources before timeout. Enforcing programmatic try...finally: sedona.stop() teardowns eliminated idle overhead completely.
  2. Decoupled Scoring Architecture: Changing MCDA weights or sigmoidal parameters never triggers multi-million polygon spatial joins.
  3. Data Fingerprinting (ETag & Snapshot Hashing): Unchanged spatial layers are skipped during batch runs, yielding a 95% compute reduction.
  4. Zero-Cost Client Offloading: Real-time multi-criteria slider adjustments execute in-browser via DuckDB-WASM and JavaScript at $0.00 cloud compute cost.

5. Conclusion & Recommendations

Adopting cloud-native distributed spatial engines (Apache Sedona on Wherobots Cloud) combined with client-side DuckDB-WASM execution solves the twin challenges of geospatial engineering: processing multi-million polygon datasets at sub-meter precision while maintaining near-zero infrastructure operating costs.

Government agencies, energy developers, and infrastructure planners can leverage this open-source template (aura_siting_crafter) to transform months of desktop GIS modeling into automated, reproducible spatial digital twins in seconds.
